Task 21: [FIX] Draw the "Region N" label outside the rectangle so it stops covering the text

You MUST read AGENTS.md at the repository root and follow its rules.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE PROBLEM - REPORTED BY THE HUMAN USER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The user's exact words:

    "Region NN の表示を枠外に表示させる。
     理由：OCR文字と重なっており、見えない為。"
    (Show the "Region NN" caption outside the rectangle, because right now it
     overlaps the OCR characters and they cannot be read.)

The purpose of this application is to read the scanned page. A caption painted
on top of the page defeats that purpose.

The cause is in `custom_gui/app.py`, in `_update_selections_ui`. The label is
passed as the CONTENT of the selection rectangle container, so it is rendered
INSIDE the rectangle, on top of the image:

        drawn_rect = ft.Container(
            border=ft.border.all(2, ft.Colors.BLUE),
            bgcolor=ft.Colors.TRANSPARENT,
            left=dx1, top=dy1, width=w, height=h,
            content=ft.Text(rect.label, color=ft.Colors.BLUE,
                            weight=ft.FontWeight.BOLD)     # <-- this
        )
        self.rects_layer.controls.append(drawn_rect)

2. DEFINITION OF DONE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[A] The rectangle no longer contains the label.
    - The selection rectangle keeps its blue 2px border and transparent fill.
    - It must have NO text content of its own. Nothing is painted over the page
      inside the rectangle.

[B] The label is drawn as a separate control, outside the rectangle.
    - Default position: immediately ABOVE the rectangle, left-aligned with its
      left edge, so the caption sits in the margin above the selection.
    - Give it a readable background (for example an opaque or high-opacity
      container behind the text) so it is legible over any part of the scan.
    - It must still be visually associated with its rectangle at a glance.

[C] It must never be clipped off the top of the image.
    - If the rectangle is near the top edge and the label would be drawn at a
      negative y, place it just BELOW the rectangle's top edge instead (still
      outside the drawn area if possible, otherwise immediately inside the top).
    - Express this decision as a PURE function so it can be tested without a GUI,
      for example:

          def calculate_label_position(rect_left, rect_top, label_height,
                                       margin=2.0) -> (label_left, label_top)

      Returning a y above the rectangle when there is room, and a fallback y when
      there is not. Choose the exact contract yourself, but it MUST be pure,
      GUI-free, and documented with a docstring.

[D] Everything keeps following the image.
    - The label must move with its rectangle when the user pans (Pan mode) and
      when the user zooms, exactly like the rectangle itself.
    - It must be positioned using the display coordinates already computed for
      the rectangle in `_update_selections_ui`, i.e. derived from
      `original_to_display(x, y, self.zoom_scale, self.offset_x, self.offset_y)`.

[E] Tests (no GUI window may be opened).
    Create `tests/test_label_position.py` with CONCRETE NUMERIC assertions, not
    property round-trips. At minimum:
      (a) A rectangle with plenty of room above, e.g. rect_top = 300 and
          label_height = 20, places the label ABOVE it: assert the exact expected
          y value your formula produces, and assert it is strictly less than 300.
      (b) The label's left edge equals the rectangle's left edge (or your
          documented offset from it) - assert the exact number.
      (c) A rectangle at rect_top = 0 does NOT produce a negative y: assert the
          exact fallback value.
      (d) A rectangle at rect_top = 5 with label_height = 20 (not enough room)
          also does NOT produce a negative y.
